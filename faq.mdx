# FAQ

Quick answers to the questions that come up most often. For long-form explanations, follow the links in each answer.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="What does Tumbuh do?" icon="leaf">
Tumbuh is a yield aggregation vault on Base. You deposit a supported stablecoin (currently IDRX), the protocol routes it through an automatic swap to the vault's underlying asset (currently USDC) and across multiple DeFi yield protocols, and pays the yield back as a steadily rising share value. When you withdraw, you receive your stablecoin equal to your proportional share of the vault — typically more than you deposited if yield has accrued.

See [How it works](/how-it-works/overview) for the full loop.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need USDC to deposit?" icon="coins">
No. You deposit your stablecoin (currently IDRX). The protocol automatically swaps a portion to the vault's underlying asset (currently USDC); you never have to hold USDC directly.

See [Tokens](/tokens) for how the entry stablecoin, the vault asset, and the receipt token relate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why do I receive a receipt token instead of vault shares?" icon="receipt">
The receipt token (currently tIDRX) is your **receipt** — proof that you deposited a specific amount of stablecoin. The actual yield-bearing position is held by the protocol on your behalf and tracked under your address.

This indirection lets you think in your stablecoin terms even though the vault accounts in a different asset. It also lets the protocol add new entry stablecoins in the future without changes to the vault.

See [Tokens → tIDRX](/tokens#tidrx) for the full explanation.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why is the receipt token non-transferable?" icon="lock">
The receipt token is tied to the protocol's per-user accounting. If you could transfer it to another wallet, the receipt and the underlying position would diverge — the new holder would have a receipt with no claim, and the original holder would have a claim with no proof. Soulbound enforcement keeps the receipt and the position bound to the same address.

To move funds between addresses, withdraw on the source address and deposit from the destination address — a round-trip that incurs swap slippage in both directions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where does my yield come from?" icon="trending-up">
The vault deploys capital across a diversified set of DeFi yield protocols. Each one earns yield on its own (lending, looped strategies, market making, or other mechanics).

Tumbuh aggregates the result into a single rising share value. **Your receipt-token balance does not rise as yield accrues** — yield lives in the vault, and you see it at withdrawal when your receipt burns for more stablecoin than you deposited.

See [Yield generation](/how-it-works/yield-generation).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who controls my funds?" icon="shield">
- **You** control whether to deposit and when to withdraw (via your wallet signing transactions).
- **The vault** holds the vault asset on-chain.
- **The protocol's entry layer** holds the vault positions attributed to you.
- **The strategy allocator** distributes capital across the integrated yield protocols.
- **Admin keys** held by the Tumbuh team can configure parameters, pause for emergencies, and upgrade contracts. There is no on-chain timelock today — admin actions take effect immediately.

See [Access control](/security/access-control) for the role matrix and [Risks](/security/risks-and-disclosures#admin-key-risk) for the residual admin-key risk.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if one yield protocol fails?" icon="triangle-exclamation">
A failure in any single integrated protocol translates to a proportional loss for Tumbuh holders. Diversification across multiple protocols reduces exposure but does not eliminate it.

The vault's value reading falls back to the protocol's last known value if its live reading fails, so a broken integration cannot block the vault. The protocol's guardian role can force-exit a misbehaving integration.

See [External protocol risk](/security/risks-and-disclosures#external-protocol-risk).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What are the fees?" icon="calculator">
**Coming soon.** The fee model is being redesigned. **No fees of any kind are charged today** — there are no entry, exit, management, or performance fees while the new model is in design. The new design will be posted here when ready.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the protocol audited?" icon="circle-check">
**Not yet.** Tumbuh has not been audited by a third-party firm. Until an audit completes, assume higher residual smart-contract risk than a comparable audited protocol. Audit progress will be posted on the [Risks and disclosures](/security/risks-and-disclosures) page when available.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I withdraw any time?" icon="arrow-up-from-line">
Yes, subject to gas and slippage. You initiate withdrawal on [app.tumbuh.fi](https://app.tumbuh.fi); the stablecoin returns to your wallet in the same transaction. There is no lockup, no withdrawal queue, and no minimum hold period.

The exception is when the protocol is paused — see [What if the entry layer stops working?](#what-if-the-entry-layer-stops-working) below.

See [Withdraw flow](/how-it-works/withdraw-flow) for the full sequence.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why did I get slightly less stablecoin back than I deposited?" icon="arrow-down">
Two sources contribute to the difference:

- **Swap rate movement.** Both deposit and withdraw involve swaps between your stablecoin and the vault asset. The market rate at execution may differ slightly from the rate when you reviewed the estimate.
- **Yield-protocol exit cost.** When the vault redeems your portion of the yield-protocol positions, an integrated protocol may pay out slightly less than its quoted value because of withdrawal fees, looped-position unwinding, or rounding.

The exit cost is **isolated to you** — it does not reduce share value for other holders. This keeps the system fair: the user who triggered the strategy exit pays the cost of that exit.

If yield has accrued long enough to outweigh both, you receive more stablecoin than you deposited. See [Why your output may differ slightly from the estimate](/how-it-works/withdraw-flow#why-your-output-may-differ-slightly-from-the-estimate).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What network is it on?" icon="network-wired">
Base mainnet (chain ID 8453). All deposits, swaps, and yield deployment happen on Base. You need a Base-compatible wallet and ETH on Base for gas.

See [Contract addresses](/developers/contract-addresses).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there a mobile UI or web app?" icon="mobile">
Yes. The official web app is at [app.tumbuh.fi](https://app.tumbuh.fi). It is the recommended way to deposit, withdraw, and check your position.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What if the website is down?" icon="globe">
Your position lives on-chain and is independent of any specific website. As long as you can submit transactions to Base, your funds are safe even if the web app is temporarily unavailable.

Developer-oriented users can interact directly with the contracts; see the [Integration guide](/developers/integration-guide).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What if the entry layer stops working?" icon="route">
Your vault position is held by the protocol's entry layer on your behalf, so a broken entry layer blocks normal withdrawals. If the entry layer is paused indefinitely, upgraded incorrectly, or otherwise unable to process withdrawals, recovery requires admin coordination.

The procedure is documented in the [Router recovery runbook](/security/risks-and-disclosures#router-recovery-runbook). This is the largest user-facing dependency in the protocol — worth understanding before depositing.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can Tumbuh support other input tokens in the future?" icon="arrow-right-arrow-left">
Yes — the protocol's entry layer is the extensibility point for additional supported stablecoins. Today the entry layer accepts IDRX. A future entry layer (or this one upgraded) could accept additional supported stablecoins without changes to the underlying vault, because the vault is denominated in the vault asset and the entry layer does the input-asset conversion.

Whether and when to add another input asset is a protocol decision, not a contract limitation.
</Accordion>

</AccordionGroup>
