# FAQ

Quick answers to the questions that come up most often. For long-form explanations, follow the links in each answer.

<AccordionGroup>

<Accordion title="What does Tumbuh do?" icon="leaf">
Tumbuh is a yield aggregation vault on Base. You deposit IDRX, the protocol routes it through a swap to USDC and across multiple ERC-4626 yield protocols, and pays the yield back as a steadily rising share price. When you withdraw, you receive IDRX equal to your proportional share of the vault — typically more than you deposited if yield has accrued.

See [How it works](/how-it-works/overview) for the full loop.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Do I need USDC to deposit?" icon="coins">
No. You deposit IDRX. The Router automatically swaps a portion to USDC behind the scenes through 0x. The Vault is denominated in USDC because the integrated yield protocols are; you never have to hold USDC directly.

See [Tokens](/tokens) for how IDRX, USDC, and tIDRX relate.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why do I receive tIDRX instead of vault shares?" icon="receipt">
tIDRX is your **receipt** — proof that you deposited a specific amount of IDRX. The actual yield-bearing position (vault shares) is held by the Router on your behalf and tracked under your address.

This indirection lets you think in IDRX even though the Vault accounts in USDC. It also lets the Router add new input assets in the future without changing the Vault.

See [Tokens → tIDRX](/tokens#tidrx) for the full explanation.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why is tIDRX non-transferable?" icon="lock">
tIDRX is tied to the Router's per-user accounting. If you could transfer tIDRX to another wallet, the receipt and the underlying position would diverge — the new holder would have tIDRX with no claim, and the original holder would have a claim with no proof. Soulbound enforcement keeps the receipt and the position bound to the same address.

To move funds between addresses, redeem on the source address and deposit from the destination address — a round-trip that incurs swap slippage in both directions.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Where does my yield come from?" icon="trending-up">
The Vault deploys USDC across multiple external ERC-4626 yield protocols — Morpho, AutoFinance, Avantis, and any other protocol added in the future. Each one earns yield on its own (lending, looped strategies, market making, or other mechanics).

Tumbuh aggregates the result into a single rising share price. **Your tIDRX balance does not rise as yield accrues** — yield lives in the vault shares the Router holds for you, and you see it at redemption when your tIDRX burns for more IDRX than you deposited.

See [Yield generation](/how-it-works/yield-generation).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Who controls my funds?" icon="shield">
- **You** control whether to deposit and when to redeem (via your wallet signing transactions).
- **The Vault** holds USDC and shares on-chain.
- **The Router** holds the vault shares attributed to you.
- **The StrategyManager** allocates USDC across the integrated strategies.
- **Admin keys** (`DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE`, `OPERATOR_ROLE`, `GUARDIAN_ROLE`, `ADMIN_MANAGER`) can configure parameters, pause, and upgrade contracts. There is no on-chain timelock today — admin actions take effect immediately.

See [Access control](/security/access-control) for the role matrix and [Risks](/security/risks-and-disclosures#admin-key-risk) for the residual admin-key risk.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What happens if one strategy protocol fails?" icon="triangle-exclamation">
A failure in any single integrated protocol (Morpho, AutoFinance, Avantis, or any future addition) translates to a proportional loss for Tumbuh holders. Diversification across multiple strategies reduces exposure but does not eliminate it.

The Vault's `totalAssets()` falls back to the strategy's last-known value if its `convertToAssets` call reverts, so a broken external vault cannot brick TVL reads. The Guardian can call `emergencyWithdrawStrategy` to force-exit a misbehaving strategy.

See [External protocol risk](/security/risks-and-disclosures#external-protocol-risk).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What are the fees?" icon="calculator">
Tumbuh charges a single **performance fee on profit above the all-time high**. The rate is bounded at 30% (3000 bps), set by the Operator. There are no entry, exit, or management fees.

The fee only applies to gains above the high-watermark — drawdowns never trigger a fee. Fees are paid in vault shares to the treasury, not deducted from your IDRX.

See [Fees and yield](/fees-and-yield) for the mechanics and a worked example.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is the protocol audited?" icon="circle-check">
**Not yet.** Tumbuh V3 has not been audited by a third-party firm. Until an audit completes, assume higher residual smart-contract risk than a comparable audited protocol. Audit progress will be posted on the [Risks and disclosures](/security/risks-and-disclosures) page when available.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can I withdraw any time?" icon="arrow-up-from-line">
Yes, subject to gas costs and slippage. Call `redeemWithSwap` and you receive IDRX in the same transaction. There is no lockup, no withdrawal queue, and no minimum hold period.

The exception is when the Router is paused — see [What if the Router stops working?](#what-if-the-router-stops-working) below.

See [Withdraw flow](/how-it-works/withdraw-flow) for the full sequence.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Why did I get slightly less IDRX back than I deposited?" icon="arrow-down">
Two sources contribute to the difference:

- **Swap slippage.** Both deposit and redeem involve IDRX↔USDC swaps through 0x. The market rate at execution may differ slightly from the quote.
- **External-protocol exit slippage.** When the Vault redeems your portion of the strategy positions, the external vault may pay out slightly less than its `convertToAssets` quote because of withdrawal fees, looped-position unwinding, or rounding.

The exit slippage is **isolated to you** — it does not reduce share price for other holders. This keeps the system fair: the user who triggers the strategy exit pays the cost of that exit.

If yield has accrued long enough to outweigh both, you receive more IDRX than you deposited. See [Slippage isolation](/fees-and-yield#slippage-isolation).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What network is it on?" icon="network-wired">
Base mainnet (chain ID 8453). All deposits, swaps, and yield deployment happen on Base. You need a Base-compatible wallet and ETH on Base for gas.

See [Contract addresses](/developers/contract-addresses).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Is there a mobile UI or web app?" icon="mobile">
Tumbuh is a smart-contract protocol. The interface you use to deposit and withdraw is built and operated separately from the protocol contracts. If you are evaluating a specific frontend or mobile app, verify it interacts with the addresses listed in [Contract addresses](/developers/contract-addresses) — no other interface is endorsed by the protocol.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What if the website is down?" icon="globe">
The website is a frontend. Your position lives on-chain — the Vault's shares, the Router's `userVaultShares` mapping, and your tIDRX balance — and is independent of any specific website. As long as you can submit transactions to Base and call the Router directly, you can deposit and withdraw without a website.

The technical-minded can call `depositWithSwap` and `redeemWithSwap` directly via Etherscan, a Foundry script, or any wallet that supports custom contract calls. See the [Integration guide](/developers/integration-guide).
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="What if the Router stops working?" icon="route">
Vault shares are held by the Router on your behalf, so a broken Router blocks normal redemptions. If the Router is paused indefinitely, upgraded incorrectly, or otherwise unable to execute `redeemWithSwap`, recovery requires admin coordination.

`DEFAULT_ADMIN_ROLE` retains the ability to call `vault.redeem` directly via the `onlyAuthorized` modifier. The full procedure is documented in the [Router recovery runbook](/security/risks-and-disclosures#router-recovery-runbook).

This is the largest user-facing dependency in the protocol — worth understanding before depositing.
</Accordion>

<Accordion title="Can Tumbuh support other input tokens in the future?" icon="arrow-right-arrow-left">
Yes — the Router is the extensibility point for additional input assets. Today the Router accepts IDRX. A future Router instance (or this Router upgraded) could accept a different stablecoin or other input asset without changing the Vault, because the Vault is denominated in USDC and the Router does the input-asset conversion.

Whether and when to add another input asset is a protocol decision, not a contract limitation.
</Accordion>
